What we believe

Wash it.Don't trash it.Every school.

Most of us have eaten off a foam tray with a plastic spork that came shrink-wrapped with a straw and a napkin. You eat for twenty minutes, then the whole thing goes in a bag, and the bag goes to a landfill where the foam sits for a few hundred years. Nobody chose this on purpose. Schools switched from washable trays to disposables decades ago because dishwashers and the people to run them cost money, and foam cost five cents.

Then the "green" fix showed up: compostable fiber trays. They cost eight to thirteen cents, they're often coated with PFAS, and in most districts there is no industrial composter to send them to, so they go to the same landfill the foam did. The school pays more to feel better.

In China, every student in a school canteen eats off the same stainless tray, hands it back, and gets it clean the next day. No one there thinks this is remarkable.

That's the whole idea. Not a new material, not an app. A standard. One tray shape, one fork and spoon, one cup, one return crate, one dishwasher cycle, in every school in a district. When everything is the same, nobody sorts, nothing runs out, and the per-meal cost drops to about a penny or two.

RePlate is the company in the middle. We buy the sets in volume from a U.S. foodservice factory, deliver them with the racks and crates, train the staff in ten minutes, and take back anything that gets bent or dented. A food-service director says yes once and never buys a tray again.

We're a student team. We eat these lunches. That's why we care about the tray.

Proof

Two districts already did this. Here's what it cost them.

Both were documented by the ReThink Disposable program (Clean Water Fund). We didn't make these numbers up; we built our model on them.

Case study · 2025

Bedford Public Schools, Massachusetts

4 schools · ~1,400 lunches a day · foam trays → stainless, December 2024

  • 261,720 foam trays gone per year, almost 3 tons of waste
  • $11,777 net savings a year on serviceware after payback
  • Payback in 9 months with 110% tray stock; 8.3 months at one tray per meal
  • About $20,700 a year less in waste-hauling fees on top
  • Per school: elementary $2,700–3,100, middle $2,300, high school $3,600 a year
The lead custodian reported trash falling from 18 bags to 6 per lunch day at one elementary school, and said the change streamlined the whole lunchtime cleanup.ReThink Disposable, Bedford case study
Case study · 2026

Arlington Public Schools, Massachusetts

4 schools of 325–500 students · plastic cutlery and fiber trays → stainless, 2022–2026

  • 315,436 disposable items gone per year, 3.6 tons
  • $12,929 net savings a year across trays, cutlery and bulk condiments
  • 7.4-month payback on the whole program
  • Cutlery alone: 215,294 plastic utensils avoided, $2,153 a year saved
  • Started with a high-school student group in 2022
The food-service director said she'd been anxious about the switch and that it turned out to be harder for the adults than for the students.ReThink Disposable, Arlington case study

Per-meal cost for a reusable = set price ÷ meals over its life, plus washing. Making a stainless tray takes more energy than making one foam tray, and the stainless one breaks even after roughly 50–100 uses, which is a few weeks of lunches.

End of life

A tray that never becomes trash

Still usable

Donated to school meal programs in Africa

Dented or scratched sets that still hold food safely are sanitized and shipped to partner school-feeding programs, where many canteens serve on single-use or shared plates.

Beyond repair

Sold as stainless scrap

Stainless is one of the most recycled materials on earth and scrap has real resale value. A cracked tray goes back into the steel supply, not into the ground.

Always

Replaced without the school lifting a finger

Replacement sets ride along with the next delivery. The school never buys serviceware piece by piece and never puts a tray in a trash bag.
